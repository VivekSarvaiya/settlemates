Moving in with friends is exciting, right up until someone asks, "So how are we splitting the rent?"

Equal shares feel like the obvious answer. Three people, ₹45,000 a month, ₹15,000 each. Done. Except one room has an attached bathroom and a balcony, and another is the small one next to the kitchen that hears the pressure cooker every morning. Six months in, the person in that room is quietly annoyed, and nobody wants to be the one who brings it up.

The good news is that there are simple, fair ways to split rent when the rooms aren't equal. Here are three that work, with real numbers you can copy.

## Decide what "fair" means before you sign

Fair doesn't always mean equal. It means everyone agrees the split makes sense, ideally before anyone has picked a room.

Have this conversation while you're still looking at flats. It's much easier to agree on a rule when nobody knows yet which room they'll end up in.

For all three methods below, we'll use the same flat:

- Rent: ₹45,000 a month
- Room A: 160 sq ft, attached bathroom, balcony
- Room B: 120 sq ft
- Room C: 120 sq ft

## Method 1: Split by room size

This works best when the main difference between the rooms is space.

Think of the rent as paying for two things: the shared parts of the flat (living room, kitchen, common bathroom) and your own room. Everyone uses the shared space equally, so that part is split equally. The bedrooms are split by size.

A good starting point is to treat half the rent as shared and half as bedrooms:

1. The shared half is ₹22,500, split three ways: ₹7,500 each.
2. The bedroom half is also ₹22,500, split by area. The bedrooms add up to 400 sq ft, so Room A (160 sq ft) pays 40% of it, and Rooms B and C (120 sq ft each) pay 30% each.

| Room | Shared part | Bedroom part | Monthly rent |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A (160 sq ft) | ₹7,500 | ₹9,000 | ₹16,500 |
| B (120 sq ft) | ₹7,500 | ₹6,750 | ₹14,250 |
| C (120 sq ft) | ₹7,500 | ₹6,750 | ₹14,250 |
| Total | ₹22,500 | ₹22,500 | ₹45,000 |

<Callout variant="tip" title="The 50/50 split is a dial, not a rule">
  If your flat has a big living room everyone actually uses, treat more of the rent as shared, say 60%. If people mostly stay in their own rooms, treat less of it as shared. The more you count as shared, the closer everyone's rent gets to equal.
</Callout>

## Method 2: Put a price on the extras

Size isn't always the issue. Sometimes the rooms are about the same, but one has an attached bathroom, a balcony or the only AC. Then it's easier to agree on what each extra is worth per month and let that set the gap between rooms.

Say the three of you agree that:

- an attached bathroom is worth ₹2,000 a month
- a balcony is worth ₹1,000 a month

Room A has both, so it should cost ₹3,000 more than the other two. Here's the part people often get wrong: that ₹3,000 is the **gap** between rooms, not an extra charge on top of the rent. The total still has to come to ₹45,000.

So B and C each pay a base amount, and A pays the base plus ₹3,000. Three base amounts plus ₹3,000 makes ₹45,000, which puts the base at ₹14,000.

| Room | Monthly rent |
| :--- | ---: |
| A (bathroom and balcony) | ₹17,000 |
| B | ₹14,000 |
| C | ₹14,000 |
| Total | ₹45,000 |

Compared with an equal split, B and C each pay ₹1,000 less and A pays ₹2,000 more. That's the whole idea: the people in the plainer rooms get a discount, paid for by whoever gets the extras.

## Method 3: Can't agree on a price? Average everyone's guess

Arguing about whether a balcony is worth ₹1,000 or ₹3,000 can go on for a while. There's a quicker way.

Everyone writes down, privately, how much more they think the best room is worth each month. Then you average the numbers. If the three guesses are ₹4,000, ₹2,000 and ₹3,000, the average is ₹3,000. Use that as the gap, exactly as in Method 2, and you get the same ₹17,000, ₹14,000 and ₹14,000.

It works because nobody has to defend their number out loud, and it's hard to game. Lowball the best room hoping to get it cheap, and you only nudge the average a little.

## Rent is only half the bill

Fixing the rent and then fighting about electricity defeats the point. Agree on everything else at the same time:

- **Electricity:** split equally, unless one person runs the AC all night and the others don't. In that case, agree on a fixed extra each month rather than measuring every unit.
- **Wi-Fi, gas cylinder, cook or cleaner:** usually equal, because everyone uses them.
- **Groceries:** keep one shared list and settle up once a month, not after every trip to the shop.
- **Security deposit:** split it in the same ratio as the rent. With the Method 2 split and a ₹90,000 deposit, that's ₹34,000 for Room A and ₹28,000 each for B and C.

The deposit matters more than people expect. When someone moves out, they'll want their share back, and that's far easier if everyone agreed on the shares on day one.

## Write it down in one message

You don't need a contract with your flatmates. You need one message in the group chat that everyone has seen:

> Rent from 1 December: A ₹17,000, B ₹14,000, C ₹14,000. Everyone transfers to whoever pays the landlord by the 3rd, and the landlord gets paid by the 5th. Electricity and Wi-Fi split equally. Deposit split ₹34,000, ₹28,000, ₹28,000.

Pin it. When someone moves out or the rent goes up, edit the numbers instead of starting the conversation from scratch.

<Callout variant="warning" title="Don't let one person float the rent">
  If one flatmate pays the landlord, everyone else should pay them before the due date, not after. Covering other people's rent for a week every month is exactly how good flatmates turn into frustrated ones.
</Callout>
