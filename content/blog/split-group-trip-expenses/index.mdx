Group trips are brilliant. The money part, less so.

On day one, everyone's generous. Someone books the villa, someone else grabs the first few cabs, and lunch gets paid by whoever reaches for their phone first. By day four, nobody remembers who paid for the scooter rentals, and a trip that was meant to be about the beach is now partly about a spreadsheet.

None of this happens because anyone is being cheap. It happens because nobody set up a system. Here's a simple one that works for trips of any size.

## Before you go: agree on three things

Five minutes in the group chat now saves hours of untangling later.

1. **A rough budget.** Say a number out loud, like "around ₹15,000 each, all in". It stops one person booking a five-star stay while another was expecting a hostel.
2. **What's shared and what isn't.** Stays, fuel, cabs and group meals are usually shared. Shopping, souvenirs and that third cocktail usually aren't. Settle the grey areas, like drinks, before the first night out.
3. **Who books what.** One person handles the stay, another handles transport. The fewer people paying big amounts, the less there is to track.

## During the trip: one note, every payment

Start one shared note, or a pinned message in the group chat, and add every shared payment as it happens: who paid, how much, and what for.

- Aisha paid ₹22,000 for the villa
- Rahul paid ₹3,200 for fuel
- Meera paid ₹1,850 for lunch at the shack

That's it. Don't try to settle each payment as you go. Twenty transfers of ₹340 are annoying to send and impossible to check later. Track everything, settle once.

<Callout variant="tip" title="What about a kitty?">
  Some groups put money into a kitty at the start, say ₹5,000 each, and pay for shared things from that. It works well for small daily costs like snacks and entry tickets. For big bookings, it's usually simpler to let one person pay and note it like any other expense.
</Callout>

## Shared doesn't always mean everyone

Three of you go parasailing and two stay on the beach. That ₹4,500 should be split between the three who went, not all five.

Keep these partial expenses on their own lines in your note, with the names of the people who joined. When you settle up, split them only among those people. Mixing them into the main pot is the most common reason trip maths feels unfair.

If you're using the SettleMates calculator, run each partial expense as its own small split with just the people who joined.

## After the trip: settle within two days

Settle up while everyone still remembers the trip, ideally within a day or two of getting home. Here's how it works for a four-person trip:

| Person | Paid for | Amount paid |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| Aisha | Villa | ₹22,000 |
| Rahul | Cabs and fuel | ₹14,000 |
| Meera | Food | ₹8,000 |
| Dev | Activities | ₹4,000 |
| Total | | ₹48,000 |

The trip cost ₹48,000, so each person's fair share is ₹12,000. Now compare that with what each person paid:

- Aisha paid ₹10,000 over the fair share, so gets ₹10,000 back.
- Rahul paid ₹2,000 over, so gets ₹2,000 back.
- Meera paid ₹4,000 under, so owes ₹4,000.
- Dev paid ₹8,000 under, so owes ₹8,000.

Then match the people who owe with the people who are owed, biggest amounts first:

1. Dev pays Aisha ₹8,000.
2. Meera pays Aisha ₹2,000.
3. Meera pays Rahul ₹2,000.

Three payments, and everyone's square. If everyone had paid each other back expense by expense, the same trip could easily have meant a dozen transfers. With four people, you never need more than three.

## Keep it friendly

- **Share the breakdown, not just the request.** Post the full split in the group so everyone can check the maths, rather than a message that just says "you owe me ₹8,000".
- **Agree on rounding.** Exact to the rupee, or rounded to the nearest ₹10? Either is fine. Deciding up front avoids a debate about ₹7.
- **Pay quickly.** The fastest way to get invited on the next trip is to settle this one the same day.
- **Thank whoever booked.** Fronting ₹22,000 for a villa is a favour, even when it's paid back.

Once the trip is settled, the only thing left to argue about is where to go next.
